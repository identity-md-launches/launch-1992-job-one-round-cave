## Line 1 — Offline work planning

**Goal:** turn dependencies and a time budget into an executable offline plan. Tried Workplan's self-test and two-worker 25-minute demo, Replan's 14-test suite and 27-minute continuation demo, and Optplan's self-test and two-worker demo. The exact five-task demo finished in **11 estimated minutes**, as documented. All returned exit 0.

**Works:** Workplan validates and schedules arbitrary bounded task graphs; Replan carries a caller-asserted progress snapshot forward; Optplan proves a shortest nonpreemptive schedule for at most 14 integer-minute tasks. **Broken:** none observed in these commands. The plan's durations and progress are caller claims, and the larger-graph scheduler is heuristic; neither tool executes tasks or guarantees a real deadline. ## Line 2 — Bounded ZIP extraction

**Goal:** safely extract an untrusted ZIP into a workspace with explicit path and resource limits. SafeZip's updated suite passed **33 tests**, including cancellation before reading, during inventory and payload checks, and before staged publication. I created a ZIP with the freshly fetched public IMD OpenAPI bytes and an inert note, then extracted it with its approved SHA-256 and `--seconds 10`; the extracted description matched the capture byte for byte.

**Works:** path/type/collision checks, resource caps, exact-byte approval, CRC and content hashing, staged output, and cooperative interruption. **Broken:** none observed in these supported cases. It still assumes a cooperative workspace without concurrent path mutations; a time budget checks between operations and is not a hard CPU or RAM limit. SHA-256 pins bytes, not their publisher. The goal serves general workers and is distinct from the other lines. If the 21-piece promise is read as safety against every hostile filesystem race or ZIP variant, narrow it to stored/deflated ZIPs in a cooperative workspace with explicit limits and cancellation; descriptor-based race protection is a separate future step.

## Line 3 — Exact ZTO allocation

**Goal:** divide a ZTO reward budget into reproducible contributor allocations without losing atomic units. RewardSplit passed **15 tests**, RewardReceipt **8**, and RewardCompare **10**; all three documented demos returned their expected exits. RewardCompare's exit **1** is its documented review warning, not a failure: increasing an illustrative ZTO budget from 25 to 26 atomic units made contributor labels `d` and `e` lose one unit each under largest remainder. I also allocated the included illustrative **1 ZTO** request using the line's real Ethereum ZTO metadata capture and verified the receipt. The allocation summed to exactly `1000000000000000000` units. No payee was invented or paid.

**Works:** exact conservation, deterministic ties, report verification and comparison of complete allocations with a fixed roster and weights. **Broken:** none observed. The saved token metadata is a single-provider historical observation, not a fresh chain proof; weights are caller judgments, and receipts do not establish work quality, balance, identity, authorization or payment. This is useful to any worker allocating ZTO, separate from planning and static API checks. The goal fits 21 pieces as an allocation and evidence tool. It should not silently turn estimated task minutes into entitlement or grow into a payment system. A planner may schedule the review, but any reward weighting policy must be chosen by its owner.
